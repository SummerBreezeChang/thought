# Competitive research, round 2: curated gifting

*Nov 5, 2026 · Round 1 (kid cards, 529 plans, remittance, red-envelope apps) is in [../RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md) §5.*

## The one-line answer

No one combines **cross-border money** with a **meaningful, curated gift**, in the **grandparent's language**, with **fees shown**, **pooled across currencies**, plus a **child view**. Each competitor has one or two of these pieces.

## Most important findings

**1. PayPal Pools is our partner's own closest product.**
- It relaunched in Nov 2024 for the US, Germany, Italy and Spain ([newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2024-11-14-PayPal-Unveils-Innovative-Money-Pooling-Feature,-Simplifying-Group-Expenses-Between-Family-and-Friends)).
- "Anyone across the globe can contribute," but only "international fees apply," with no amount stated ([PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/us/digital-wallet/send-receive-money/pool-money)).
- **The pitch:** Thought is what Pools looks like when it's built for one family moment. It adds curation, the grandparent's language, a fee breakdown, and a child view. That shows PayPal judges a direction for their own product rather than a competitor.

**2. Hidden FX is a real pattern.**
- Riley & Grey quietly takes "a standard 2% exchange rate" from the recipient when a guest gives in another currency ([help](https://help.rileygrey.com/article/144-how-do-i-add-a-cash-fund-to-my-registry)).
- Babylist sends fee and international questions off to Venmo/PayPal ([help](https://help.babylist.com/hc/en-us/articles/218112247-How-do-I-add-a-Cash-Fund-to-my-registry)).
- Group-gift money is often locked:
  - Babylist store credit "cannot be converted to cash."
  - Amazon eGift cards can't be returned or transferred.

**3. Wise is the transparency pattern to match.** You send X → fee Y → mid-market rate Z → they get W, plus arrival time ([Wise vs PayPal](https://wise.com/us/compare/paypal-vs-wise)). Our cost receipt follows this pattern and adds what the money becomes, for example "a third of an Exploratorium pass."

**4. Delivering experiences and care credits is possible.** Real giftable products exist that we can point to as future fulfillment partners:

**5. People are asking, and no product answers.**
- Japanese grandparents ask on Yahoo Chiebukuro whether to ship from Japan or buy on Amazon US for a toddler in America ([example](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q13166097128)).
- A German asks how to gift DoorDash to a cousin near SF ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/doordash/comments/qv92ky/how_to_buy_gift_card_from_different_country/)).
- No product is built for grandparents abroad gifting to US grandkids.
- **The closest competitor is SodaGift** ([sodagift.com/ja](https://sodagift.com/ja)): Japanese-language, cross-border gift cards, but no fee/FX breakdown, no pooling, no kids, no curation.

**6. Regulation is a pitch angle.** The CFPB Remittance Transfer Rule protects senders *in the US* ([Reg E §1005.30](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/30/)), so gifts *into* the US from Taiwan or Japan aren't covered. Our line: **"We give remittance-grade fee disclosure voluntarily, even though no US law requires it for inbound gifts."** Get a legal check before claiming more.

## White space

1. Cross-border tools move value with no feeling. Gifting tools are US-only or English-only.
2. Nobody curates SF-specific gifts and explains them in Japanese or Mandarin.
3. Pooling across currencies with each person's conversion shown is unsolved.
4. Care credits for parents are never framed as "Grandma gave you a night off."
5. Kids have no lasting record of who gave what, in that person's own voice.

## Pitch lines

1. "Wise shows Grandma exactly what her yen becomes. Babylist shows her a crib. Thought is the only place she sees both, in Japanese."
2. "Everyone else gives you a gift card and a currency surprise. We give you an Exploratorium pass the whole family chipped in for, with every fee shown before anyone pays."
3. "Gift cards say 'from Grandma.' Thought lets your kid hear her say it."

## Not verified

- Foreign-card acceptance at Babylist, Zola, Amazon group gifts, ActivityHero, UrbanSitter and all four museums.
- The size of PayPal Pools' international fee.
- Caribu pricing and Uber's redemption limits (from third-party sources only).
